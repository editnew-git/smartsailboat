[Smart Sail Boat](https://smartsailboat.com) is testing a workflow intended to turn field-recorded video into a YouTube upload and a companion public story. In this onboard recording from Mazatlán, Mexico, the participants describe the test as an effort to make documentation more accessible to people with limited computer skills.

The proposed process begins with recorded video. During the current test, Zoom provides a way to capture footage without relying entirely on storage on the recording phone. The team discusses a future version in which someone could record on a phone or tablet, upload the file to a shared folder and then work with an agent to prepare the YouTube video and story. Those later steps are plans under evaluation, not completed capabilities demonstrated by this recording.

The conversation also records an early operational lesson: handling several videos at once made the previous workflow slower and harder to review. The participants therefore propose working one original video at a time while they continue testing uploads, generated delivery artwork, review and publication approvals.

A broader goal is to build an indexed public knowledge base from field documentation and, eventually, let visitors ask questions across that material. The speakers also discuss testing whether the workflow can operate off grid with limited solar power. The recording does not establish that either capability has been completed or proven.

The video shifts from the onboard discussion to a walk near the dock, where the participants collect water and describe living aboard while repairs continue. They mention that the boat currently lacks refrigeration and that the toilet needs repair. Waterfront views show the bay and open ocean as the speakers describe future travel after the sailboat is fixed. No voyage or repair completion is claimed.

This original, uncut recording documents a work-in-progress test: what the participants are trying, what made the earlier attempt difficult and what they plan to evaluate next.
